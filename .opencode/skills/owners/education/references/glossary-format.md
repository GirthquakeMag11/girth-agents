# Workspace glossary format

`GLOSSARY.md` is the learner's own language for the topic. Every lesson,
exercise, and learning record speaks its terminology. Building it is part of
learning: compressing a concept into a tight definition is itself evidence
of understanding.

```markdown
# {Topic} Glossary

{One or two sentences on the topic this glossary covers.}

## Terms

**<Term>**:
<One or two sentences: what it is.>
_Avoid_: <rejected synonyms, comma-separated>
```

The rules:

- **Add a term only when the learner understands it.** The glossary records
  compressed knowledge, not a reading list. Until the learner can use a
  concept correctly, it waits.
- **Be opinionated.** Where several words exist for one concept, pick the
  best and list the rest under `_Avoid_`. This is how the language
  compresses.
- **Keep definitions tight.** What the thing *is*, in one or two sentences.
- **Use the glossary inside itself.** Once a term exists, prefer it in later
  definitions.
- **Group under subheadings** when natural clusters emerge; a flat list is
  fine while terms cohere.
- **Flag ambiguities explicitly.** Where a word is used loosely in the wider
  field, note the resolution: "In this workspace, 'set' always means a
  working set."
- **Revise as understanding deepens.** A week-one definition may be wrong by
  week six; update in place, never leave it stale.
