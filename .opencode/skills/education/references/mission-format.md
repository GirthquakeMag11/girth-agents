# MISSION.md format

`MISSION.md` lives at the workspace root. It captures the *reason* the
learner is taking this on; every teaching decision traces back to it.

```markdown
# Mission: {Topic}

## Why
{1-3 sentences. The concrete real-world goal. What changes in the
learner's life or work when they have this skill? Push for the outcome,
not "to understand X".}

## Success looks like
- {A specific, observable thing the learner will be able to do}
- {Another}
- {…}

## Constraints
- {Time, budget, prior commitments, learning preferences}

## Out of scope
- {Adjacent topics the learner is not chasing right now — protected so the
  zone of proximal development stays on target}
```

The rules:

- **One mission per workspace.**
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Ship a Rust CLI to my team" beats "learn
  Rust."
- **Push back on vagueness.** No good answer to *why* means a mission
  interview before anything else — a bad mission is worse than none.
- **Revise when reality shifts.** The mission moves when the learner
  discovers what they actually want; update it rather than letting a stale
  compass steer sessions.
- **Keep it short.** Past a screen it has stopped being a compass and
  become a plan.
